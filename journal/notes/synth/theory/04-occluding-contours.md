### Single body — the occluding contour taxonomy of $\partial B^\infty$

Let $\partial B^\infty$ be [[jpk-aspectg-surfaces#Genericity — generic surfaces and general position (printed p.233–234, §2)|generic]] and fixed in a scene. Let $c$ be a camera center in space imaging $\partial B^\infty$ through [[03-projective-geometry#Central projection (perspective/pinhole camera model)|central projection]]. An [[jpk-aspectg-surfaces#Aspect graph — definition (printed p.231, §1)|aspect-graph]] partitions space into connected components. Let $c$ lie in the interior of such a component then it is a [[koen-door-sin-vis#Stable vantage point — Definition (paper p.54, §2.3.1)|generic (stable) camera centre]]. The boundary separating components is determined by [[jpk-aspectg-surfaces#Aspect graph — definition (printed p.231, §1)|visual-events]]. Note that almost all camera centres are [[koen-door-sin-vis#Stable vantage point — Definition (paper p.54, §2.3.1)|stable]]; conversely unstable ones, marked by visual events, are measure zero. Because $c$ lies off the surface, [[cip-vis-mot#Perspective projection onto the unit sphere (paper p.1105)|central projection]] from $c$ is a [[gupo-diff-top#Smooth maps (book p.1–2)|smooth map]] $P_c:\partial B^\infty\to$ image; because $c$ is stable, its singularities are [[jpk-aspectg-surfaces#Codimension; stable vs accidental viewpoints (printed p.234, §2.1)|generic]]. That singular set — the points where the visual ray grazes the surface — is the [[03-projective-geometry#Occluding contour generator|contour generator]] $\Gamma$, and its visible image is the [[03-projective-geometry#Occluding contour (apparent contour)|apparent contour]] $\gamma$. The two questions of this section are: from a fixed generic $c$, *what can $\gamma$ look like*; and as $c$ moves, *how can $\gamma$ change*. Both have a finite, complete answer.


The components of $\Gamma$ are [[contesse#Setting — closed surfaces, generic position (§3)|closed curves]].
The <abbr title="Don't read 'components' in a strict mathematical sense — the curves may intersect thereby connecting them">components</abbr> of $\gamma$ are curves, of three kinds: 
- the **silhouette** — [[ben-her-line-draw#The silhouette (paper p.42)|the outermost closed curve]] in the image plane bounding the projection of $B^\infty$; smooth and closed.
- interior **closed** curves — fully visible [[ben-her-line-draw#Chaining — closed loops vs arcs ending at cusps/T-junctions (paper p.87, §9.4)|closed loops]] with no spine and no T-junction.
- interior **open** curves — smooth curves that [[ben-her-line-draw#Chaining — closed loops vs arcs ending at cusps/T-junctions (paper p.87, §9.4)|begin/end at spines or T-junctions]] .
	- A **spine** is local: the [[koen-door-sin-vis#Cusp / spine — where the fold-locus is asymptotic (paper p.54, §2.3.2)|curve ends]] because $\Gamma$'s tangent swings onto the line of sight, and the surface folds over to hide its own continuation (the pre-image is a hyperbolic point).
	- A **T-junction** is multilocal: the curve ends because a nearer part of the surface [[koenderink-solid-shape#The T-junctions (p.445)|occludes it]] — [[koen-door-sin-vis#T-junction and global events — creation / annihilation of T-junction pairs (paper p.56-57, §2.4)|a transversal crossing of two folds]], two surface points at the same image point.

Within a connected component of the aspect-graph, $\gamma$ is [[koen-door-sin-vis#Stable vantage point — Definition (paper p.54, §2.3.1)|topologically equivalent]]. Meaning the curves deform continuously as $c$ moves within a component, but no contour generator/spine/T-junction is created, destroyed, or recombined. Observing this topological change is what [[jpk-aspectg-surfaces#Aspect graph — definition (printed p.231, §1)|marks a visual-event]].

**Proposition (Apparent-contour taxonomy of $\partial B^\infty$).** For a generic smooth closed surface and a generic camera centre, $\gamma$ is the union of closed curves and open curves beginning/ending at a **spine** or **T-junction**. As $c$ moves, the topology of $\gamma$ is constant except across isolated transitional viewpoints — the **visual events**. The [[jpk-aspectg-surfaces#Codimension; stable vs accidental viewpoints (printed p.234, §2.1)|codimension]]-1 events, the ones a moving camera generically crosses, are exactly six: three *local* ([[cipgi-vis-mot#Lips and beaks — visual events (book p.96–97)|lips, beaks]], [[cipgi-vis-mot#Swallowtail — visual event (book p.96)|swallowtail]]) and three *multilocal* ([[jpk-aspectg-surfaces#Multilocal visual events — tangent crossing, cusp crossing, triple point (printed p.237, §2.3)|tangent crossing, cusp crossing, triple point]]). This list of six is the complete codimension-1 catalogue ([[jpk-aspectg-surfaces#The six visual events (printed p.236, end of §2.3)|Petitjean–Ponce–Kriegman, 1992]]); higher-codimension events exist but are not generically encountered.

*Construction.*
1. **Only two stable map singularities.** $P_c$ is a smooth map between $2$-manifolds, and [[koen-door-sin-vis#Fold-locus and outline — Whitney's two map singularities (paper p.54, §2.3.2)|Whitney's theorem]] says such a map has exactly two stable singularity types, the **fold** and the **cusp** ([[koen-door-sin-vis#Singularities are of two types — folds and cusps; a complete event list (abstract, p.51-52)|Koenderink & van Doorn, 1976]], after Whitney 1955). Every stable feature of $\gamma$ is therefore one of these or a crossing of them.

2. **Fold $\to$ smooth arc.** At a generic point of $\Gamma$ the singularity is a fold: $\gamma$ is the smooth image of the fold-locus, locally separating visible from occluded surface, and "the fold-locus is everywhere smooth" ([[koen-door-sin-vis#Fold-locus and outline — Whitney's two map singularities (paper p.54, §2.3.2)|KvD §2.3.2]]). Hence away from the isolated points of steps 3–4, $\gamma$ is a union of smooth arcs.

3. **Cusp $\to$ contour ending.** At the special points where the direction of $\Gamma$ is an [[cipgi-vis-mot#Asymptotic direction — Definition 2.8.11 (book p.35)|asymptotic direction]] — equivalently the visual ray has [[cipgi-vis-mot#k-point contact — §2.13 (book p.49)|3-point contact]] with the surface — the fold degenerates and $\gamma$ shows a **cusp** ([[koen-door-sin-vis#Cusp / spine — where the fold-locus is asymptotic (paper p.54, §2.3.2)|KvD "spine"]]; [[cipgi-vis-mot#Static taxonomy — generic view (Ch 3, §3.4–3.5)|Cipolla–Giblin Property 3.5.2(i)]]). Only one branch is visible, so for an opaque surface $\gamma$ simply ends in a contour termination ([[jpk-aspectg-surfaces#Stable singularities — Whitney's fold and cusp; the opaque reduction (printed p.233, §2)|Petitjean–Ponce–Kriegman, 1992]]); the pre-image is a [[cipgi-vis-mot#Elliptic / parabolic / hyperbolic — Definition 2.8.13 (book p.36)|hyperbolic point]]. The cusp is **stable** — it persists under camera motion.

4. **T-junction $\to$ occlusion crossing.** Where projection superimposes one part of the surface on another, two fold arcs cross transversally in a **T-junction** ([[koen-door-sin-vis#T-junction and global events — creation / annihilation of T-junction pairs (paper p.56-57, §2.4)|KvD §2.4]]; for an opaque surface the image double point becomes the t-junction with one branch terminating, [[jpk-aspectg-surfaces#Stable singularities — Whitney's fold and cusp; the opaque reduction (printed p.233, §2)|PPK, 1992]]); the surface condition is a [[cipgi-vis-mot#Bitangent → image crossing — §3.4 (book p.60)|bitangent ray]]. "The visibility of one branch ends at the T-junction," so the through-branch is the nearer one and the junction carries a **local depth ordering**.

5. **The stable catalogue is exactly $\{$fold, cusp, T-junction$\}$.** At a [[koen-door-sin-vis#Stable vantage point — Definition (paper p.54, §2.3.1)|stable vantage point]] the set of critical values is topologically locked, and steps 1–4 are the only types KvD admit. So for generic $c$, $\gamma$ is precisely smooth fold arcs, ending at cusps, crossing at T-junctions — answering the first question.

6. **Events occur only at unstable vantages, and only finitely many types.** Moving $c$ keeps the vantage stable except at isolated viewpoints where the topology of $\gamma$ jumps — a **visual event**. KvD prove only a few event types are possible and give "a complete list" ([[koen-door-sin-vis#Singularities are of two types — folds and cusps; a complete event list (abstract, p.51-52)|KvD, abstract]]), in two families.

7. **Local events = creation/annihilation of a cusp pair** ([[koen-door-sin-vis#Local events — creation / annihilation of cusp pairs (paper p.54–55, §2.3.3)|KvD §2.3.3]]). For a smooth surface these are the three codimension-1 local types ([[rig-class-view#Local projection singularities — Table 2 (paper p.94)|Rieger Table 2]]; named and defined by [[jpk-aspectg-surfaces#Local visual events — lip, beak-to-beak, swallowtail (printed p.236–237, §2.3)|Petitjean–Ponce–Kriegman, 1992]], after Kergosien 1981):
   - [[cipgi-vis-mot#Lips and beaks — visual events (book p.96–97)|Lips]] — a small closed loop of $\gamma$ is born/dies at a point; $c$ crosses the asymptotic rays at [[cipgi-vis-mot#Elliptic / parabolic / hyperbolic — Definition 2.8.13 (book p.36)|parabolic]] points, elliptic side.
   - [[cipgi-vis-mot#Lips and beaks — visual events (book p.96–97)|Beaks]] — two arcs meet and reconnect, $\Gamma$ momentarily singular; same surface, hyperbolic side.
   - [[cipgi-vis-mot#Swallowtail — visual event (book p.96)|Swallowtail]] — an arc grows two cusps with a crossing between them; $c$ crosses the [[cipgi-vis-mot#Flecnodal point / curve / scroll — §2.13 (book p.50–51)|flecnodal scroll]] (4-point contact).

8. **Multilocal events = creation/annihilation of a T-junction pair** ([[koen-door-sin-vis#T-junction and global events — creation / annihilation of T-junction pairs (paper p.56-57, §2.4)|KvD §2.4]]; [[jpk-aspectg-surfaces#Multilocal visual events — tangent crossing, cusp crossing, triple point (printed p.237, §2.3)|PPK §2.3]]). For a smooth surface these are three, matching Rieger's codimension-1 [[rig-class-view#Multilocal singularities — superimposed apparent contours (paper p.94)|superimposed-contour types]] `5+5+5`, `5⧺5`, `5+6`:
   - [[jpk-aspectg-surfaces#Multilocal visual events — tangent crossing, cusp crossing, triple point (printed p.237, §2.3)|Tangent crossing]] (`5⧺5`) — two fold points project onto the same image point and share a common tangent.
   - [[jpk-aspectg-surfaces#Multilocal visual events — tangent crossing, cusp crossing, triple point (printed p.237, §2.3)|Cusp crossing]] (`5+6`) — a cusp and a fold point project onto the same image point.
   - [[jpk-aspectg-surfaces#Multilocal visual events — tangent crossing, cusp crossing, triple point (printed p.237, §2.3)|Triple point]] (`5+5+5`) — three fold points project transversally onto the same image point.

1. **No other events, because $\partial B^\infty$ is smooth.** The remaining [[jpk-aspectg-surfaces#Aspect graph — definition (printed p.231, §1)|aspect-graph]] events are of higher codimension or are of the [[aa-vis-pws#Multilocal visual events — triple point, tangent crossing, vertex crossing (paper p.4–5)|vertex-crossing]] types , which need a crease vertex where surface-normal discontinuities meet; a smooth closed surface has none. The smooth-approximation result above removes creases even from the cube, so the smooth catalogue applies with no edge/corner events. The three stable features (steps 2–4) are thus the generic catalogue of $\gamma$, and the six events (steps 7–8) the complete codimension-1 catalogue of its transitions. $\square$

**Remark (Geometry is encoded in the contour).** The contour reports surface curvature, which is what lets a believed shape be checked against an observed $\gamma$. Where $\gamma$ is a smooth fold, the sign of its image curvature tracks the sign of the [[do-carmo-diff-geom#Gauss map (book p.135)|Gauss curvature]] $K$ along $\Gamma$; an inflexion of $\gamma$ marks a [[cipgi-vis-mot#Elliptic / parabolic / hyperbolic — Definition 2.8.13 (book p.36)|parabolic]] point ($K=0$), and a cusp marks an asymptotic ray (hence locally hyperbolic, $K<0$) ([[cipgi-vis-mot#Static taxonomy — generic view (Ch 3, §3.4–3.5)|Koenderink's formula, Property 3.5.3]]; [[koen-occ-con|Koenderink, 1984]]). We use this qualitatively.

### Multiple bodies — inter-object occlusion

The single-body taxonomy extends to a scene of many bodies almost unchanged, because the projection theory it rests on is *multilocal*. Take the scene to be finitely many disjoint [[jpk-aspectg-surfaces#Genericity — generic surfaces and general position (printed p.233–234, §2)|generic]] smooth closed surfaces $\{\partial B_i^\infty\}$, with disjoint union $S=\bigcup_i\partial B_i^\infty$ — itself a compact smooth $2$-manifold, now disconnected. Central projection $P_c:S\to$ image is still a [[gupo-diff-top#Smooth maps (book p.1–2)|smooth map]] of $2$-manifolds, and the generic classification — Whitney's local singularities plus the multilocal events — never assumed the singular pre-image points lie on the *same* component. The catalogue is therefore closed under disjoint union. [[malik-lines|Malik (1987)]] states this at source level: occlusion of a surface "by another object (or another part of the same object)" is one phenomenon producing one feature — the [[malik-lines#T-junctions arise from occlusion — depth ordering (§7, printed p.81)|T-junction]].

**Proposition (Apparent-contour taxonomy of a multi-body scene).** For finitely many disjoint generic smooth closed surfaces and a generic camera centre, the apparent contour is the visible union of the per-body single-body contours, and the only feature coupling distinct bodies is the **inter-object T-junction**. The stable catalogue is unchanged — $\{$curve, spine, T-junction$\}$ — and so is the list of six visual events, the three *multilocal* ones now additionally admitting inter-object instances. Each T-junction carries a local depth order, and the set of all T-junctions cuts $\gamma$ into one component per body.

*Construction.*
1. **Per-body contours are untouched.** [[03-projective-geometry#Front-facing and back-facing|Front/back-facing]] is decided in an arbitrarily small neighbourhood of a surface point, so each contour generator $\Gamma_i$ and its fold arcs are exactly those of $\partial B_i^\infty$ imaged alone. Other bodies enter only through visibility of the projection, not through $\Gamma_i$.

2. **Smooth scenes admit only two junctions.** For scenes where each object is bounded by single smooth surface, "the only junctions in the line drawing would be **T-junctions and terminals**" ([[malik-lines#Smooth objects → the only junctions are T-junctions and terminals (§8.2, printed p.83)|Malik §8.2]]). A **terminal** is the cusp/contour-ending ([[malik-lines#Terminal = cusp; nearer patch is recoverable (§8.2, printed p.81)|Malik §8.2]]), already present for a single body. Hence the **only feature multiple bodies add is the T-junction**. 

3. **No crease junctions, because $\partial B^\infty$ is smooth.** Malik's full catalogue for piecewise-smooth surfaces — Terminal, L, Curvature-L, Three-Tangent, Arrow, Y, T ([[malik-lines#The junction catalog for piecewise smooth surfaces (§11, Fig. 17, printed p.89)|Malik Fig. 17]]) — collapses to $\{$Terminal, T$\}$: L, Curvature-L, Three-Tangent, Arrow and Y each require an edge or vertex (a tangent-plane or curvature discontinuity), and the smooth-approximation result above removes creases even from the cube.

4. **A T-junction carries a local depth order.** "The top of the T-junction corresponds to a nearer surface occluding another object… there is no constraint on the label of the stem" ([[malik-lines#T-junctions arise from occlusion — depth ordering (§7, printed p.81)|Malik §7]]): the **crossbar** is the nearer (through) branch, the **stem** the occluded one. For an *inter-object* T-junction this is a depth order *between two bodies*. The collection of all T-junctions gives the scene's pairwise occlusion relation; it is a partial order (an occlusion DAG) when the bodies are depth-separable, but **not in general** — two non-convex bodies can each occlude the other in different image regions, so the order is read locally at each junction, not assumed globally.

5. **T-junctions also segment the scene.** Malik's labelling algorithm begins: "Split the ISG at T-junctions and find connected components… in scenes with multiple objects, there would be several components with distinct objects belonging to distinct components" ([[malik-lines#Object segmentation by splitting at T-junctions (§14, printed p.94)|Malik §14]]). So $\gamma$ alone does **not** determine the number of bodies; the T-junctions are simultaneously the depth cues and the cuts that group contour into objects — the object-segmentation problem is read off them.

6. **Events under camera motion: the same six, now inter-object.** With the surfaces held *constant* and only the camera moving, the single-body analysis applies verbatim to the disjoint union. The local events (lips, beaks, swallowtail) are per-surface, unchanged; the three multilocal events — [[jpk-aspectg-surfaces#Multilocal visual events — tangent crossing, cusp crossing, triple point (printed p.237, §2.3)|tangent crossing, cusp crossing, triple point]] — now additionally admit *inter-object* instances, the coincident fold/cusp points lying on different bodies. One body's contour sweeping in front of another creates or annihilates a T-junction pair — KvD's interposition event ([[koen-door-sin-vis#T-junction and global events — creation / annihilation of T-junction pairs (paper p.56-57, §2.4)|KvD §2.4]]). The catalogue of event *types* is unchanged. (Relative motion *between* bodies drives these same events and is treated with the dynamics below; here the bodies are fixed.) $\square$

**Remark (finite resolution breaks the catalogue).** The catalogue is an infinite-precision statement. Under limited resolution nearby T-junctions merge and the labelling fails ([[malik-lines#Finite resolution merges T-junctions (§16, printed p.100, Fig. 38)|Malik §16]]) — the multi-body analogue of the standoff/scale-space caveat that lets us treat $\partial B^*$ as $\partial B^\infty$ in the first place.
