### Time dimension — dynamics, and what motion adds

Everything above is a single instant. Time adds a clock $t$ and sets the scene moving. We state the moving scene and ask, of each thing time adds, what it *introduces, changes, challenges, or leaves constant* — never promising to integrate the evolution law we write down.

#### The time-extended scene
Carry the stereo setting forward — multiple surfaces $\{\partial B^\infty_i\}$ seen by the [[06-stereo#The canonical configuration|canonical rig]] — and index everything by $i$. Each scene object is $O_i=(B_i,X_i,F_i)$: the body $B_i$ in its own coordinates, the non-body state $X_i$ (pose and the rest) that places it in the world, and an evolution law $F_i$. Following [[temam-inf-dim-dynsys#Abstract evolution equation (book p.3, §1, General Introduction)|Temam]], write it as one abstract ODE $\dot u_i = F_i(u_i)$ on a phase space $H_i$ carrying the object state $u_i=(B_i,X_i)$: a rigid object lives in finite-dimensional $H_i=\mathbb{R}^N$, a deforming one in an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space . Objects couple through an **index set** $C(i)$ recording who acts on whom — not all-to-all. The agent is itself a body: the left camera center traces $c(t)$, the right rides a fixed offset $b$ within it, and the rig moves as one rigid body with pose $g(t)\in SE(3)$.

The image sees only the **composition** of agent motion and world motion — relative motion — and in general the two are coupled, not separable a priori. The assumption that breaks the deadlock is that **ego-motion is known**: subtract the agent's known contribution and the residual is world motion. That single grant is what makes the rest tractable, and it recurs as the section's organizing lever.

The natural division follows from where dynamics live: in the *derivatives*. The law $F_i$ and the coupling $C(i)$ are inert at any single instant and act only across an interval. So we split **instant vs interval**. At an instant, time adds nothing — every single-instant result survives untouched. Across an interval it adds a genuinely new class of observable: **rates of change** — velocity, the motion field — quantities that did not exist in the static problem at all.

#### Agent moves, world frozen
The classic moving-camera case. Translation opens a **second depth channel** beside the baseline — **parallax** — and makes the dense per-pixel **motion field / optical flow** ([[sze-cv#Optical flow — dense per-pixel motion (§9.3, printed p.578)|Szeliski §9.3]]) available, readable off the slope of pixel tracks through the stacked spatio-temporal volume ([[sze-cv#Multi-frame motion estimation — the spatio-temporal volume (§9.3.3, printed p.587)|Szeliski §9.3.3]]); fusing matches over the sequence grows the *effective* baseline (temporal / multi-baseline stereo). Correspondence becomes **two-axis** — **spatial matching** ($L\leftrightarrow R$) and **temporal matching** ($t\leftrightarrow t{+}1$) — and the epipolar geometry fixed in the static rig is now time-varying, though the rig's *internal* geometry stays rigid: only $g(t)$ moves. Viewpoint change sweeps **visual events** along the trajectory (aspect-cell crossings; [[bowyer-aspect-moving|Bowyer et al., 1993]]), and two single-instant deferrals come due — **motion blur** from finite exposure ([[sze-cv#Shutter speed = exposure integration (§2.3, printed p.81)|Szeliski §2.3]]) and **temporal aliasing** from sampling $t$ at the frame rate, the dual of the spatial aliasing already noted. Even with the world frozen, the **frustum sweeps**: which surfaces fall in view is $g(t)$-dependent, so the visible set undergoes **birth and death from ego-motion alone** — points enter and leave frame, and self-occlusion boundaries shift with viewpoint.

This *challenges* the temporal axis: data association must hold over time and resist **drift**; structure and ego-motion are coupled unknowns (the structure-from-motion problem, [[sze-cv]] Ch. 11); and forward motion is degenerate — the focus of expansion enters the frame and disparity turns radial. What stays **constant** is the load-bearing part: the intrinsics $K$, the baseline $b$, and the instantaneous two-view relation. Each single time-slice is *exactly* the [[06-stereo#Two-image formation — the stereo configuration and the depth it recovers|static stereo problem]].

#### World moves too
The half the literature usually omits. Now the object states $(B_i,X_i)(t)$ evolve — rigid motion, articulation, deformation — bodies move relative to one another through the coupling $C(i)$, and the visible set gains a **world-driven** birth/death on top of the ego-driven one: inter-object occlusion and disocclusion, marked at the T-junctions that carry depth order ([[malik-lines|Malik, 1987]]). The motion field now superposes two causes, ego-induced and object-induced.

The decisive change is that **temporal triangulation is no longer valid.** Triangulation needs all rays to meet at one 3D point ([[sze-cv#Triangulation — 3D point from rays + known cameras (§11.2.4, printed p.701)|Szeliski §11.2.4]]); but a tracked point's position is now a function of $(B_i,X_i)(t)$, so between two times it has *moved*, the rays no longer intersect, and every across-time fusion that assumed it held still — multi-baseline stereo, monocular SfM, optical flow — is biased. Brightness constancy weakens too, as appearance changes with viewpoint *and* deformation.

Here the known-ego grant pays off. Predict the image motion a *static* point would produce; whatever flow remains is the **residual**, and the residual is object motion — so the ego/scene ambiguity that looks fundamental collapses into thresholding a residual ([[sze-cv#Layered motion — motion segmentation (§9.4, printed p.589)|motion segmentation, Szeliski §9.4]]). And one thing stays **constant** through all of it: **synchronized stereo still triangulates.** The frozen-instant argument never referred to the world being still, so it survives world dynamics intact.

#### The implicit static-world assumption
Most computer vision — optical flow, rigid SfM, bundle adjustment, temporal stereo fusion — assumes a static world. It is rarely written down, and it is false for a live scene. Naming it splits the machinery into two legs:

- **Instantaneous (spatial / stereo).** At a single instant the world *is* static — both cameras freeze it together — so stereo triangulation, contour and [[06-stereo#The occluding contour and frontier points|frontier]] geometry, and the perspective model all carry over unchanged, **spending no assumption at all.** 

- **Over an interval (temporal).** Here dynamics are real and the static-world assumption is the false shortcut. We spend the **known ego-motion** instead: a temporal method becomes a static-world *prediction* whose residual is the environment's dynamics. The same tools move from "broken under motion" to "a dynamics detector" — most of the world *is* static over a short interval, so the prediction holds almost everywhere, and it is precisely where it fails that the signal lives.

One caveat keeps this honest. The "single instant" is an idealization: it holds exactly only under perfect left/right synchronization and zero exposure. Finite exposure and **rolling-shutter** readout ([[sze-cv#Rolling vs. global shutter (§2.3, footnote, printed p.80)|Szeliski §2.3]]) smuggle a small time window back into the "spatial" measurement, so stereo's immunity to dynamics degrades smoothly with motion speed rather than being absolute — the design reason a fast platform wants a global shutter.

Adjacent roads (named, not taken here): **scene flow** — joint recovery of 3D shape and a per-point 3D velocity field, the literature's home for "the world isn't static" ([[sze-cv#Scene flow — joint 3D shape + 3D motion (§12.7.1, printed p.785)|Vedula et al.; Szeliski §12.7.1]]); **dynamic SLAM** (e.g. DynaSLAM), which detects and excises moving objects so a static-world estimator survives — the pragmatic dual of treating dynamics as signal; **non-rigid SfM** (Bregler et al., 2000), a low-rank shape basis for deformable $B_i(t)$; and the **dynamical aspect graph** ([[bowyer-aspect-moving|Bowyer et al., 1993]]), which lifts visual events from viewpoint alone to relative motion.
